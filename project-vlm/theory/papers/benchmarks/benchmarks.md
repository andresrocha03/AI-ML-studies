# Benchmarks
Some datasets relevant to test VLM models.

- [GQA: A New Dataset for Real-World Visual Reasoning and Compositional Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09506)
  - Visual reasoning questions on real-world photographs of everyday environments.
  - Images: taken from COCO and FLICKR datasets. 
  - Questions: open-ended, but short and well defined answers are expected (a single word, even yes or no in some cases).
  - Question generation:
    - Uses scene graphs of from Visual Genome, human made.
    - Creates question patterns ready to be filled in with scene graph attributes.
    - Combine different relationships to create questions that require several steps.
    - Adjusts the distribution of questions and answers through sampling.
  - Evaluation: Use of answer exact-matching accuracy.
  - Problems:
    - Data leakage: Images were taken from existing datasets.
    - Vulnerable to scene graph annotation mistakes.

- [MME: A Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13394)
  - Visual reasoning questions on a diverse set of images.
  - Images: taken from:
    - COCO and other existing datasets.
    - Manually taken
    - Generated by diffusion models.
    - Each image has 2 Yes or No questions. The accuracy in a subtask is the sum of both of them into a final metric that goes up to 200.
    - Subsets: percepetion and cognition. 
      - Inside each one of these subsets, specific tasks are also evaluated. Some that may be relevant for autonomous driving scenarios are: Existence, Count, Position, Color, OCR and Commonsense reasoning.
  - Question generation: they were all manually designed.
  - Evaluation: answer exact-matching accuracy.
  - Problems:
    - Yes or no questions could lead to 50% accuracy by guessing.
    - Data leakage: there are images that come from other existing datasets.
    - Synthetic images generated by diffusion models may not be reliable. (However, since all questions were manually designed, it is reasonable to say that the images were reviewed.)
  - Others: It is relatively small.


- [COCO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.09506)
  - Common objects in Context.
  - Images:
    - Photographs of everyday scenes
    - Collected from FLICKR, online platform where users upload photographs.
  - Captions generation: manually written.
  - Evaluation: caption evaluation metrics
    - BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr
  - Problems:
    - Pruning methods visual information losses are difficult to be detected since reference captions focus on most relevant parts of the image. That means that the model may focus on a single image aspect and receive a high score for that.
    - Correct captions can receive low scores if they use a diferent vocabulary from reference captions.
    - Incorrect captions may receive a high score if it uses the correct ngrams.
    - Data leakage: COCO has been available since 2014, it is possible that it has been used to train VLMs.

- [Flickr30k](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.04870)
  - People and animals performing activities in natural, real-world environments.
  - Images:
    - Photographs of everyday scenes.
    - Collected from FLICKR, online platform where users upload photographs.
  - Captions generation: manually written.
  - Evaluation: caption evaluation metrics
    - BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr
  - Problems:
    - Pruning methods visual information losses are difficult to be detected since reference captions focus on most relevant parts of the image. That means that the model may focus on a single image aspect and receive a high score for that.
    - Correct captions can receive low scores if they use a diferent vocabulary from reference captions.
    - Incorrect captions may receive a high score if it uses the correct ngrams.
    - Data leakage: it has been available for a while, it is possible that it has been used to train VLMs.

- [Pope](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10355)
  - After being fine tuned in MSCOCO, VLMs may be prone to generate frequently appearing objects in MSCOCO.
  - Images:
    - Come from MSCOCO dataset.
    - Use of object annotations present in COCO.
  - Questions:
    - Yes/No questions about the presence of a specific object in the image.
    - Use an automatic question generation pipeline to make questions given the objects in the scene.
    - Also select objects not present in the image and build questions about it.
    - 6 questions per image.
  - Evaluation:
    - Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1 and "Yes" ratio.
  - Problems:
    - It does not cover hallucinations involving object attributes, quantities and positions.

- [V*Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14135)
  - High-resolution photographs of complex real-world scenes.
  - Images:
    - Selected from SA-1B (photographs of the real-world).
  - Questions:
    - Demand for locating and describing small details in the image.
    - Spatial relationship reasoning questions as well.
    - Multiple-choice questions.
    - Questions were manually designed
  - Evaluation:
    - Accuracy.
    - The probability of candidate answers is computed and the highest one is the selected alternative.
  - Limitations:
    - Very small - 191 samples.
    - Multiple choice questions allow guessing.
    - Image preprocessing could influenciate results in case needed for the model's input low resolution.

- [HR-Bench: Divide, Conquer and Combine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15556)
  - Designed to evaluate fine-grained visual perception in high-resolution images.
  - Images:
    - High-resolution real-world images, including complex scenes, maps and charts.
    - Collected from DIV8K and the Internet.
    - Two versions: HR-Bench 8K and HR-Bench 4K, containing 200 samples each.
    - The 4K images are obtained by cropping the 8K images around question-relevant objects.
  - Questions:
    - Multiple-choice questions.
    - Two main categories:
      - Fine-grained Single-instance Perception (FSP): attribute recognition, OCR and visual prompting.
      - Fine-grained Cross-instance Perception (FCP): spatial relationships, map analysis and chart analysis.
  - Question generation:
    - Human annotators identify relevant objects and annotate their bounding boxes.
    - Relevant image regions are cropped and provided to GPT-4o to generate question-answer pairs.
    - Human experts review and filter incorrect or ambiguous questions.
  - Evaluation:
    - Multiple-choice accuracy.
    - Cyclic permutation: answer options are reordered across multiple evaluations, and the final accuracy is averaged to reduce answer-position bias.
  - Problems:
    - Small dataset, limiting statistical reliability when comparing methods with similar performance.
    - Visual information may already be lost during image resizing before token pruning is applied.
    - The 4K and 8K versions differ in image content and relative object size, not only resolution, because the 4K images are cropped around relevant objects.
    - Multiple-choice questions may be answered correctly through guessing or language priors, despite cyclic permutation.
    - AI-assisted question generation may introduce biases from GPT-4o, although human verification mitigates this problem.
    - Some tasks require mathematical reasoning or world knowledge, making it difficult to isolate visual perception errors.
    - Potential training–test overlap because images originate from publicly available sources.

- [CountQA: How Well Do MLLMs Count in the Wild?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06585)
  - Designed to evaluate object counting capabilities in complex real-world environments.
  - Images:
    - 1,001 photographs of everyday indoor and outdoor scenes.
    - Manually captured by the authors rather than collected from existing datasets.
    - Scenes containing high object density, visual clutter and partially occluded objects.
    - Images classified as focused or cluttered according to the presence of visual distractions.
  - Questions:
    - 1,528 open-ended questions requiring integer answers.
    - Direct enumeration of a single object type.
    - Compositional counting involving multiple object types.
    - Ground-truth counts range from 0 to 400, with a median of 9.
  - Question generation:
    - Ground-truth object counts manually established at the moment of image capture.
    - Annotators could inspect the physical scene from multiple angles to resolve occlusions.
    - Counts subsequently verified using the photographs.
    - Gemini 2.5 Flash used to generate natural-language questions from the annotated counts.
    - Answers directly derived from the human-established ground truth.
  - Evaluation:
    - Exact Match accuracy: predicted count must equal the ground truth.
    - Relaxed Accuracy at 5% and 10%: predictions within the corresponding relative-error tolerance are considered correct.
    - Zero-shot evaluation with integer-only responses and temperature set to zero.
    - Gemini 2.5 Flash used to extract numerical answers from responses that do not follow the required format.
  - Problems:
    - Very low baseline performance for some VLMs may produce a floor effect, making additional degradation caused by token pruning difficult to detect.
    - Uneven distribution of object counts, with relatively few high-count examples. Overall accuracy may hide failures in densely populated scenes.
    - Visual clutter and object count are confounded: cluttered images contain fewer objects on average than focused images.
    - Counting errors may originate from visual perception, instance differentiation or enumeration errors, making it difficult to isolate the effects of visual token pruning.
    - Image resizing may eliminate fine-grained visual information before pruning is applied.
    - LLM-based answer extraction may introduce evaluation errors when responses contain multiple numerical values.
    - Limited geographic and cultural diversity due to the manual image collection process.
    - Limited scope of numerical reasoning, focusing on enumeration rather than comparisons or more complex mathematical operations.
  
- **[PerceptionBench: Evaluating Atomic Visual Perception in Multimodal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24957)**
  - Designed to evaluate individual visual perception capabilities independently of reasoning and external knowledge.
  - Images:
    - Diverse visual domains, including natural images, documents, charts, scientific diagrams, GUI screenshots and remote sensing imagery.
    - Collected from existing benchmarks, public web sources and internally curated data.
    - Released benchmark contains 3,000 image-question samples.
    - Images are compared against large-scale pretraining corpora using ISC descriptors. Images with cosine similarity above 0.95 are discarded to mitigate potential training–test overlap.
  - Questions:
    - Short, uniquely determined reference answers. Includes both open-ended and multiple-choice questions.
    - Evaluates ten atomic perceptual capabilities.

  - Question generation:
    - Frontier VLMs are evaluated on 42 existing benchmarks to identify their failures.
    - A stronger multimodal model analyzes the reasoning trajectories and identifies the earliest error responsible for each failure.
    - Error labels are clustered into a taxonomy, whose perception branch defines the ten evaluated capabilities.
    - Human annotators decompose original questions into simpler sub-questions isolating specific perceptual capabilities.
    - 1,800 questions are derived from existing benchmark failures, while 1,200 are newly authored using supplementary images (all questions manually written).
    - Questions consistently solved by an ensemble of frontier models are removed.
    - Automated verification and independent human validation ensure capability alignment, visual grounding and annotation correctness.
  - Evaluation:
    - Overall and per-capability accuracy.
    - GPT-oss-120B judges generated responses against reference answers.
    - Additional consistency evaluation through repeated inference runs:
      - Pass@4: questions answered correctly in at least one of four runs.
      - Pass⁴: questions answered correctly in all four runs.
  - Problems:
    - Atomic perceptual performance does not directly predict performance on complex downstream tasks requiring reasoning or external knowledge.
    - The benchmark's high difficulty may produce a floor effect for smaller VLMs, potentially hiding additional degradation caused by visual token pruning.
    - Image deduplication reduces but does not eliminate potential training–test overlap.

- **[BabyVision: Visual Reasoning Beyond Language](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.06521)**
  - Foundational visual abilities that humans develop during childhood, minimizing reliance on linguistic knowledge and semantic priors.
  - Images:
    - Visual puzzles, geometric figures, mazes, repeated patterns and spatial reasoning exercises.
    - Task taxonomy inspired by children's textbooks, visual development tests and developmental psychology.
    - Images collected from the Internet using reverse image search and keyword-based retrieval.
    - 388 unique images in the final benchmark.
  - Questions:
    - 135 multiple-choice questions and 253 fill-in-the-blank questions.
    - Four main categories covering 22 task types.
  - Question generation:
    - Manually written by trained human annotators.
    - Each annotation includes a question, ground-truth answer and detailed solution process.
    - Two independent experts verify answer correctness, lack of ambiguity and reliance on visual information.
    - Questions are revised or discarded when experts disagree.
  - Evaluation:
    - Accuracy based on semantic equivalence between predicted and ground-truth answers.
    - Qwen3-Max is used as an automatic judge.
    - Avg@3: average accuracy across three independent inference runs.
  - Problems:
    - Very low baseline performance for smaller VLMs may produce a floor effect, making additional degradation caused by visual token pruning difficult to detect.
    - Small dataset, particularly when divided into its 22 task types.
    - Several tasks require visual reasoning and mental transformations in addition to perception, making it difficult to isolate visual information loss.
    - Visual exercises differ substantially from natural photographs and real-world environments, limiting conclusions about downstream applications such as autonomous driving.
    - Potential training–test overlap because the images originate from publicly available internet sources.
    - Textual output may underestimate visual reasoning capabilities for tasks that are more naturally solved through drawing or visual manipulation.
  - Others:
    - BabyVision-Gen extends the benchmark to visual generation, requiring models to express solutions through image annotations rather than textual answers.
